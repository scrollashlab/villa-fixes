**Rule that reproduces all 692.** The canvas is `lround(stored · render_scale / float32(meta.scale))` per axis, where:
- `stored` and `meta.scale` come from the mesh the catalog names in `creation_info.derived_from`. That is the published `<id>-on-<volume>-<µm>.tifxyz` for 688 volumes and `tifxyz_normalized` for 4.
- `render_scale` is `scale · 2^-group-idx` from the same catalog provenance: scale is "1" for all 688; group-idx is 0 for 577 and 1 for 111, and those 111 are exactly the 111 `-L1.zarr` volumes.
- Affine, crop and scale-segmentation are empty in all 692 provenance records.
- The formula lines are identical at all five villa commits the renders were built from: 530584554c (378 volumes), 650bd566 (150), 862bd172 (148), bc28564d (11), ae2ecce9 (1).

## Why #1727 could not reproduce them

- **Its inventory skipped the transformed meshes.** `scripts/inventory.py` in github.com/ge-al/vesuvius-cpu-repro @ 113bbb9 keeps only mesh directories whose name has no `.`. The transformed meshes are `*.tifxyz`, so `audit/pairs.json` compares volumes only against `tifxyz_normalized` / `tifxyz_original` / `tifxyz_flattened` / `tifxyz`.
- **The renders used the transformed mesh as-is.** They did not apply the affine before sizing: the affine is empty in every provenance record.
- **Transformed meshes are regridded per axis.** xframe verified this 902/902: each axis gets `round(n · factor)`, with a factor measured separately per axis. The canvas therefore has no single isotropic scale relative to the normalized grid.

The issue's own example shows this. PHerc0139 `20250108000000-w025_2025010863` on 1.129 µm:
1. Normalized grid: 354 × 331.
2. xframe regrid factors (8.5096, 8.5081) give the published transformed mesh 3012 × 2817. xframe predicted this, and the x.tif header confirms it.
3. At group-idx 1 that gives a canvas of 30120 × 28170 (H × W). This equals the published canvas, and it explains the 4.2542 vs 4.2553 implied scales that #1727 reports.

## What this does and does not show

- **Shows.** Every published surface volume's grid size follows exactly from the mesh named in the catalog, with the documented renderer formula and the catalog's recorded render parameters. #1727's 366 are not unexplained; they were rendered from the per-axis-regridded transformed meshes.
- **Does not show** that the rendered voxels are correct. We checked canvas sizes, not pixel content.
- **On "nothing in the bucket says which mesh":**
  - It is true of the volume's `.zattrs`. All 692 carry only `canvas_size`, `chunk_size`, `multiscales`, `note_axes_order`, `num_slices`, `source_group`, `source_zarr` and `slice_step`; PR #1901 proposes adding provenance there.
  - It is not true of the bucket-root `metadata.json` as of 2026-10-07, which names the mesh and the render parameters for all 692.
  - We cannot tell whether `metadata.json` carried these fields on 2026-09-07, when #1727 was measured.
- **Not used for any conclusion.** The x.tif `Last-Modified` is later than the catalog render date for 169 meshes. S3 `Last-Modified` reflects the copy into the open-data bucket.

## Practical consequence

To size labels or masks for a published surface volume without downloading it, use:
- the mesh in the catalog's `creation_info.derived_from`, not `tifxyz_normalized`;
- `render_scale = scale · 2^-group-idx` from its provenance.

villa's Python canvas helper (`full_resolution_extent`, from #1699) then gives the exact canvas.
